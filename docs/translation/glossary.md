# Spanish terminology glossary: Medicaid Is Changing

**Purpose.** This is the starting point for the Spanish version of the site. It lists every policy, benefits, medical-legal, and proper-name term found in the English pages, with a proposed Spanish term and where that term comes from. A professional translator must review it before the pages are published.

**Prepared:** 23 Nov. 2026, from the English text of `index.html`, `keep-your-medicaid.html`, `am-i-exempt.html`, `get-care.html`, `clinics.html`, `who-to-call.html`, `about.html`, `assets/js/quiz.js` and `assets/js/visitor-count.js`.

## How to read the table

- **Where it appears** uses short file names: `index`, `keep` (keep-your-medicaid), `exempt` (am-i-exempt), `care` (get-care), `clinics`, `call` (who-to-call), `about`, `quiz.js`, `visitor.js`. "All" means the shared header, footer or phone strip on every page.
- **Source** gives a key such as [S1]. The full URLs are in the source list just below. Every source listed was fetched, and the proposed term (or the alternative named) was seen in it. "unverified" means no reputable Spanish source was found for that term, and the proposal is the author's judgment.
- **Confidence.** High: an official Illinois letter or a federal Spanish site uses the term, and it is plain. Medium: there are sources but they disagree, the wording is not plain, or the evidence is indirect. Low: unverified, or the sources found are weak. Ordinary words and UI strings with no terminology risk (for example "Paso 1", "Ver mis resultados") are marked High even when no source was looked up.
- **Official names rule.** Names people will see in English on letters, phone menus and the ABE website (for example Manage My Case, ACA Adults, ABE, Moms & Babies) stay in English, with a Spanish gloss in parentheses the first time they appear on a page. The table gives the gloss. Example: `Manage My Case ("Administrar mi caso")`.

### A warning about the HFS Spanish materials

The HFS letter people actually received, the **Spanish ACA Adult notice of 1 Sept. 2026 [S1]**, is a fairly careful human translation. It is the main anchor for this glossary. Most of the other HFS Spanish PDFs (the H.R. 1 toolkits and webinar slides [S2]-[S6], [S10]) are clearly **machine translated** and contain serious errors, for example:

- "Obra" for *Work*; "Por qué Esto Asuntos" for *Why This Matters*; "Cartón de la Palma" for *palm card* [S2].
- "al menos a media hora" (*at least half an hour*) for *at least half-time* school [S2], [S3].
- "LAS NORMAS DE MEDICAMENTO" (*medicine*) instead of Medicaid [S7]; "SEGURO DE ENFERMEDAD" and "QUEBRAR" (*to break*, for *screener*) [S10].
- Spain-only words: "supervivientes", "acogida", "coste", "la Seguridad Social".
- Phone number typo "1-877-805-5132" (should be 5312) [S3], [S5].

So: follow HFS for **official names and the words on letters**, but do not copy general wording from the toolkits, and **never copy a phone number from a Spanish HFS PDF**. Take numbers from the English HTML.

## Priority items for translator review

Each item has a one-line reason and names the Spanish the pages now use. Items are ordered by risk to the reader. The full, page-by-page version is in `translator-notes.md` ("Top priority").

1. **asylee → "personas con asilo" (used on every page), never "solicitante de asilo".** HFS's own Spanish fact sheet and flyer (S7, S8) say "solicitantes de asilo" lost Medicaid, but asylum *applicants* are still covered by Illinois's state-funded AATV program (S4, S9).
2. **"medical group" on letters (Low).** Pages use `"medical group" (grupo médico)`. The Spanish letter wording could not be seen. Check a real Spanish HFS eligibility notice.

### Regional variation (Mexico and Central America vs Caribbean vs Spain)

- **ER:** "sala de emergencias" (US) vs "urgencias" (Mexico; risk of confusion with *urgent care*).
- **high school:** "secundaria" means grades 7-9 in Mexico. Say "high school (preparatoria)".
- **green card:** "tarjeta verde" (official) vs "la green card" or "la mica" (Mexican colloquial).
- **survivors:** "sobrevivientes" (Latin America) vs "supervivientes" (Spain). Avoid Spain-only forms found in HFS toolkits: "coste", "optar a", "acogida", "la Seguridad Social", "vosotros" verb forms.
- **apply:** "solicitar" (standard) vs "aplicar" (US anglicism, widely understood). Use "solicitar".
- **bill:** "factura" vs "cuenta" (both understood).
- **SNAP:** "estampillas" (common in Mexican-American usage) vs "cupones".
- **Caribbean readers (Puerto Rico, Cuba, Dominican Republic)** are a smaller share of this audience. None of the proposed terms should be unusual for them, but this was not checked with Caribbean readers.
